# How to Contribute

Thank you for taking the time to read this guide! We believe that learning how to collaborate on open-source projects is a valuable experience for every developer.

Over the years, this course has benefited greatly from contributions made by undergraduate students. Many of our labs and projects have been built upon the work of students who came before you. Without their enthusiasm and dedication, this course would not be what it is today.

Now, it's your turn to make a difference!

To encourage you to contribute, we offer the following incentive:

**Students enrolled in AIE210006.01 or CS10005.02 can earn 3 bonus points toward their final course grade by making a qualifying contribution following these guidelines.**

## Found a Bug?

If you encounter a bug or notice something that could be improved, we'd love to hear about it!

1. **Search existing issues.** Check the repository's GitHub Issues page to see whether the problem has already been reported.

2. **Open a new issue.** If no existing issue covers your concern, create one describing the problem as clearly as possible. Our TAs will review your issue and may offer suggestions or ask for additional information.

## Want to Fix It Yourself?

Even better! We encourage you to get involved and submit your own fixes.

Here's how to get started:

1. **Discuss your approach.** Before diving into the code, describe your proposed solution in the relevant issue. Our TAs will review your proposal and let you know whether it looks reasonable. This helps you avoid spending time on changes that might not be accepted (though it's a common experience in real-world open-source development).

2. **Fork the repository.** Create your own fork of the course repository on GitHub.

3. **Create a branch and make your changes.** Work on a dedicated branch rather than directly on your main branch.

4. **Open a Pull Request.** Once your changes are ready, submit a Pull Request (PR) to the course repository. Our TAs will review your work, provide feedback, and may ask you to make further changes before merging it.

Don't worry if your first contribution isn't perfect. Review, feedback, and iteration are all part of the open-source development process.

We look forward to your contributions, and we hope you'll enjoy making this course better for future students!
